This is what the course looks like for a student.

Gia is a second-year BCA student at Jain. Her uncle Shivanna grows vegetables on four acres near Kunigal.

The power for his pump comes at night, so at 2 a.m. he walks to the field with a torch to switch it on. If he sleeps instead, weak power can burn the motor.

Gia is our example student. The crisis she takes on is real: farm power that comes only at night, and pump motors that burn. The prices are real too.

Week 1: First Offer

She defines the problem.

She names the problem in one sentence and writes a first offer from her own research, marking what she still has to check. Gia's: her uncle's night walk, solved with a box on the pump's switch and an app, Rs 4,000 plus Rs 100 a month.

She ends the week with Offer v1.

Week 2: Ask the Market

She asks the market, cheaply, online.

Before spending money on trips to farms, she listens to farmers on YouTube, in the news and on social media. Gia finds burnt motors come up as often as night walks, and a switch that protects the motor already sells from ₹400.

She ends the week with Offer v2 and a list of people to reach.

Week 3: Reach Out

She collects evidence from real people.

She sends five requests a day and has her first real calls. Gia learns a farmer either walks out at 2 a.m., or sleeps and risks the motor. Her mentor Varun tells her why the cheap switch hasn't spread: If the mechanic doesn't fit it, the farmer won't buy it.

She ends the week with Offer v3, fitted by the mechanic farmers trust.

Week 4: Validate

She validates the problem.

Validating means checking the problem with the people who live it, until they say "yes, exactly".

Gia goes back to the people she reached in Week 3: Ramesh, Krishnappa and Raju, the mechanic. Together they find the one cause: at night nobody can see the pump when the power turns weak. Ramesh reads her problem document: Yes, this is exactly what happens.

She ends the week with a problem document a farmer agreed with.

Week 5: Business Model

She builds a business that can actually run.

It has to be feasible (someone can build and fit it) and viable (it pays). Gia's: a switch that turns the pump off when the power turns weak, fitted by Raju. A pilot on three farms, free for a season, then about 10% of the repair money it saves.

She ends the week with her business on one page and Offer v4.

Week 6: Next Steps

How do we support her so she grows?

Udyaan gives her a mentor and shows her where to find help.

Her mentor Varun asks what happens if the switch cuts the pump wrongly at 2 a.m. So her first build only messages her uncle when the power turns weak. Raju fits it, and a licensed electrician checks the wiring.

She ends the course with a project file, her next task, and a mentor.

The problem

Every week of the course moves her one step on the same problem. Her uncle now gets a message from his pump instead of guessing in the dark.

How we built your rubric points into the course

We started from your points and shaped every day around them. First, the rubric that came out of them. Then each point, and a real day that shows it at work.

Your points became five scored skills.

Each skill is scored from 1 to 10, on five levels. A student who only turns up stops at 4.

  • Problem Findingnames the problem in one sentence.
  • Evidence Seekingtells Known from Guess, with real quotes.
  • Business Model Senseputs a number and a payer on the price.
  • Communicating and Askingsays the offer in the customer's words.
  • Agencystarts unchased, and comes back after a miss.

How we make sure the AI helps students

The AI does the slow parts, so students spend their time on farmers. It never decides what counts.

It helps.

A coach on each day knows today's lesson and the student's own work. It asks one question at a time, four turns at most. Helpers sort what students paste into tables and draft their messages.

Day 6, Week 2: Gia pastes what she found

3 videos3 news stories1 agri entrepreneur

She presses Sort it. The page asks with her problem in it:

What do farmers say about walking to the field at night to switch on the pump? And what do agri entrepreneurs say about it?

The sort comes back: Power comes at night, so they water at night. One farmer nearly stepped on a snake in the dark.

It's kept honest.

A quote gets a tick only if it's found word for word in what the student pasted. Plain code does all the counting and the points. The AI never gives a score.

What the student sees after a sort

✓

A farmer's sentence found in her paste. It counts.

✗

A sentence that isn't in her paste. No tick, no points.

✓

A call counts only with six or more of the person's own words saved.
