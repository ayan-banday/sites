Managerial Accounting, Unit 2: budgets, read on Nvidia

Budgeting is risk management

What is a budget actually for?

Nobody knows next year's sales. A budget decides which losses a company can live with, and which it must never take. Every line is a risk decision.

NextWho says risk works this way?

Two investors, one rule about risk

Who says risk works this way?

Both say the loss to fear is the one you can't come back from. Marks tells a company when to be careful. Naval tells it how much to bet.

Howard Marks Oaktree Capital

When: the market's mood swings between fear and greed

"We can ride out volatility, but we never get a chance to undo a permanent loss."

Risk Revisited, 2014

Naval Ravikant AngelList

How much: never so much that one loss ends the game

"The one thing you have to avoid is the risk of ruin."

How to Get Rich, nav.al

NextSo what does a company refuse to put at risk?

The pivot: protect

Nvidia protects its R&D, even in a bad year

So what does a company refuse to put at risk?

Its future. In FY23 Nvidia's sales went nowhere, and it spent 39% more on R&D anyway. The bad year was when it bet hardest on the next one.

"Much more likely that the upside is unlimited, and the downside is limited."

Naval Ravikant, nav.al. R&D is that bet: the budget caps the cost, the upside has no cap.

Source: Nvidia annual reports (10-K), FY22 to FY26. FY26 ended in January 2026.

The swing: bend

Nvidia re-plans its costs every quarter

If R&D can't move, what can?

Day-to-day costs. A plan that only looks three months ahead gives a surprise no time to grow. In the last two quarters, costs landed within about 1% of plan.

Plan: the outlook Nvidia gives investors at the start of each quarter. Costs: operating expenses. Source: Nvidia CFO commentary, Q1 and Q2 FY27.

NextThen where can a budget like this still break?

The weight: bet

The budget broke on inventory, not costs

Then where can a budget like this still break?

In bets placed at the top of the swing. Nvidia ordered chips for demand that never came, and wrote off $2.17B in FY23. The cost lines were fine; the bet wasn't.

"You can't predict. You can prepare."

Howard Marks, memo title, 2001

Source: Nvidia annual reports (10-K), inventory provisions, FY22 to FY26.

NextSo how big should any one bet be?

Naval: how much

Size every bet to survive losing it

So how big should any one bet be?

Six people each play Russian roulette once: five get rich. One person plays six times: never. A company is the one player, every quarter, so the average can't save it.

"What is true for 100 people on average isn't the same as one person averaging that same thing 100 times."

Naval Ravikant, "Kelly Criterion: Avoid Ruin", nav.al

NextAre Nvidia's own bets that small?

Naval: how much

The Anthropic bet is sized to survive

Are Nvidia's own bets that small?

This one is. Nvidia's stake in Anthropic, up to $10B, is about two and a half weeks of its sales. If it went to zero, Nvidia keeps playing.

Up to $10B against FY26 sales of $216B: 4.6%. Source: Anthropic, Microsoft and Nvidia announcement, Nov 18 2025; Nvidia FY26 10-K.

NextAnd the bets that never show up as a cost?

Marks and Naval: when and how much

The $279B in promises is not sized to survive

And the bets that never show up as a cost?

Nvidia has promised its suppliers 2.8 times the cash it holds, right when Marks sees money "round-tripped" between AI players: the pendulum near greed. Nvidia is playing roulette now.

If demand holds, sales pay the bill. If the swing turns, the bill still comes.

$279B promised to suppliers against about $99B of cash and marketable securities, plus a guarantee of up to $105B on an OpenAI company's data centre lease. Source: Nvidia 10-Ks FY23 and FY26, 10-Q Q2 FY27; Howard Marks, "Is It a Bubble?", Dec 2025.

So what is a budget, really?

Risk management: every line is a bet. Marks says when to be careful; Naval says how much to bet. Budget the promises too.

Which line in a budget could cause a loss that can't be undone?

"whenever the pendulum is near either extreme, it is inevitable that it will move back toward the midpoint sooner or later."

Howard Marks, On the Couch, 2016

"Don't risk everything."
