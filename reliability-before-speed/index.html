§5 Business ethics and social responsibility

One promise behind it all: it does what it says

HONESTY

Tell the client the real cost of a fix. Document every fault. Admit your own failures.

FAIR PRICING + QUALITY

Know the real cost → quote a price you can hold for 12 months. My keyboard isn't gonna kill me today.

EMPLOYEES

Shield them from blame. Full documentation. Respect the technicians' skill.

CUSTOMERS

Two users: the buyer and the person who uses it. He sides with the user.

“Human safety is paramount… I would never compromise the trust with the customer, the end user.”

It all boils down to common sense. Will this hurt someone?

Is that ethical? I mean, it could be. Engineers rarely see the end use.

§5 Social responsibility and advice

His hardest question: whose job does my robot replace?

THE WORRY

His robot could replace event gig workers.

WHAT HE DID

Took their gigs. Did their work.

WHAT HE DECIDED

Build a tool that works with their skill, not one that replaces them.

Public safety: robot demos hide failures: a robot that nearly struck a girl, a car that blocked an ambulance.

Sustainability: repairable + reliable → longer life, less waste. He reused a garden cart.

HIS ADVICE · ETHICS + PROFIT
  1. Satisfy owner and worker → you're going to actually make more money
  2. Reliable before fast
  3. Refuse unsafe clients: someone's gonna have to answer for it
  4. Budget for safety from day one
  5. Do no harm to people. Make sure your products do what they say they do.
