How I learn a new field

How I learn: a list of problems I'm solving.

I don't learn a field by studying topics. I pick a number I want to hit, then solve the problems in the way, one at a time. The example on this page is my friend Mohit's website.

Scroll, about 2 minutes

Step 1: a real outcome

How do I decide what to learn?

I start with a number I either hit or miss. For Mohit's website, that's 50k visitors a month. If I miss it, what I learned hasn't worked yet.

My outcome
50k visitors a month
Yes or no. "Get better at marketing" never tells me when I'm wrong.

Step 2: one real project

Where do I practise?

On one real project: Mohit's website. I try whatever I learn on it the same week. Real visitors show me what works.

My project
Mohit's website
Real visitors, a friend counting on me, and an answer within days.

Step 3: the problems

What stands between me and 50k?

I list the problems in the way, then start with the one the others depend on. I can't help people find the site until I know who it's for.

Stop 1, circled
Who's it for?
Every other problem waits on this one.

Step 4: the fields

Which fields do I need?

50k visitors takes four fields: technology, marketing, design and how people think. Each problem needs one of them. I learn the big ideas of each, and link them up.

A latticework of mental models
4 fields, 1 goal
Charlie Munger's name for knowing the big ideas of many fields and linking them.

Step 5: one problem at a time

How do I learn one problem?

I go round the same six steps every time. I learn the expert's word last, once I already get the idea. I study about an hour at a time and explain it back to an AI tutor.

Why the word comes last
Words hide gaps
Learn the word first, and I feel like I understand before I do.

Step 6: reality checks it

How do I know I've learned it?

I check the number. If visitors went up, I move to the next problem. If not, I go back and find what I got wrong, and that mistake becomes a rule I follow next time.

The test
Did visitors go up?
Yes: next problem. No: back to this one.

The whole route

Why does this work for me?

The number keeps me honest. I can feel like I'm learning a lot and still not be closer to 50k.

One problem at a time keeps it light. I only learn what the next problem needs.

I use it the same week, so I remember it.

What would your number be?

Pick one thing you want to learn. Write a number you could hit in three months, and the first problem in the way. That's enough to start.
