How do we get people to really talk to a form?

A form that listens, then tells you what people want.

How I see voice working on Chatform's big questions: what the student sees while talking, and what you get back. Each idea comes just before its screen.

Step 1 · the big question

Why do big questions get one-word answers?

Show what a good answer sounds like, then make talking the easy choice.

Three small example questions under it, and a mic that fills half the screen. "Type instead" is always there for a bus or a classroom.

Why: people go short when they can't tell what kind of answer is wanted. Examples show the size of an answer.

Step 2 · while they talk

How does the form ask for more without cutting people off?

Wait for a breath, then ask about the one thing still missing.

Chips light up as Riya covers each part of a full answer. At a 2-second pause, one short follow-up slides in, and she answers it in the same recording.

pause → one follow-up

Why: a good listener waits for you to breathe before asking "why that?". Follow-ups only ask about unlit chips, two at most, so nobody feels questioned.

Step 3 · what the owner gets

How do we see what people really want without reading every answer?

Find the want under the want, in their own words.

Chatform reads every spoken answer for the specific thing each person cares about, groups them with their quotes, and lets you ask the answers anything, like a normal chat.

Why: nobody wants "an internship" for its own sake. They want something specific it gives them. That specific thing is what you design for.

For the engineer · how I see voice

What do we build first?

Build it in this order. Each step works on its own.

  1. The big-question screenExamples, the big mic, type instead. Save the transcript.
  2. "Here's what I heard"One summary line from the transcript, with "That's right" and "Add more".
  3. Chips and follow-ups while talkingEach question carries its goal and 2 to 4 parts. At a 2-second pause, ask about one unlit part. Two follow-ups at most.
  4. Insights and "ask your answers"Group spoken answers by the specific want, with quotes.
